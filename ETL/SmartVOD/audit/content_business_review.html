Correction: SEO title history is normal

Earlier interpretation of the legacy Conflicting log titles status was too strict. Different titles over time can be normal SEO edits. They remain one host/video-key asset and do not count as a quality failure or block promotion-test recommendations. The latest observed label and prior aliases are retained.

Only missing/unverified labels now contribute to title review: 28.95% of estimated hours in this snapshot. The earlier 92.7% combined review conclusion should not be used.

Business focus

The work is now organised around content asset demand and the next review or experiment. Audience, geography and ISP metrics support asset interpretation. They are secondary to the content worklist.

Content decisions is the default Insights tab. Use Review last 7 days, inspect an exact host/key, and review its momentum, known-ID reach, request-derived minutes per known ID, repeat-day activity, delivery and audience mix. High demand is not itself proof of profitability or completion.

Immediate priorities

  1. Maintain programme IDs and title history. Verify missing/unverified labels and catalogue type/version when needed; ordinary SEO renames require no remediation.
  2. Review source categories separately from an authoritative genre/language taxonomy.
  3. Investigate material asset declines using event timing, content availability and delivery evidence; avoid automatic removal decisions.
  4. Run controlled promotion tests only after quality/reach checks; add impressions/clicks for conversion and lift.
  5. Add runtime and player telemetry for completion/drop-off, and rights/cost/revenue inputs for financial decisions.
